# A Bicycle for the Mind — NHD 2027 Website Draft

A draft National History Day website about Apple's innovations, built for the
2027 theme **Innovation in History: Impact, Influence, Change** (Junior Division,
Individual Website).

Open `index.html` in any browser to view it. Everything (pages, styles, drawings,
word counter) lives in that one file.

## Pages

| Page | What it covers |
|---|---|
| Home | Title, thesis, and the entry info NHD requires on the home page |
| Background | Room-sized computers, the Altair 8800, the Homebrew Computer Club |
| Innovation | Apple II (1977), Macintosh (1984), iPod and iTunes (2001–03), iPhone and App Store (2007–08) |
| Impact | Short-term effects and influence on competitors (Windows, Android, BlackBerry) |
| Change | Long-term change (smartphone ownership 35% → 91%) and the costs |
| Conclusion | Why it matters |
| Process Paper | Template with prompts. **You write this one.** |
| Bibliography | Starter annotated bibliography, primary and secondary, in MLA |

## NHD website rules this draft follows

- 1,200 visible student-composed words max. The draft uses about 950, so you
  have room. Quotes, image credits, menus, the process paper, and the
  bibliography do not count.
- Multimedia (audio and video) totals 3:00 or less. The draft plans 1:45.
- The home page shows your name, division, category, word count, process paper
  word count, and multimedia length.
- Process paper: 500 words max.
- The final site must be built in **NHDWebCentral** (nhd.org/nhdwebcentral).
  Treat this file as your blueprint and rebuild it there page by page.

Check your teacher's or state's current rule book, since details can change.

## Before you submit: your to-do list

Turn on the **Builder notes** button (top right of the site) to see these
reminders next to the parts they apply to.

1. **Rewrite every paragraph in your own words.** NHD rules say students may not
   let AI write their text or make their images. Use this draft to see how the
   pages fit together and which facts to research, then write it yourself. Judges
   will ask you about your argument in your interview.
2. **Put your real name** on the Home page.
3. **Replace every drawing with a real, credited photo.** These drawings are
   placeholders. Good places to look: the Computer History Museum's online
   collection, the Smithsonian's National Museum of American History, the IBM
   Archives, and Apple Newsroom press images.
4. **Add the two video clips** (the 1984 Macintosh commercial and the 2007
   iPhone introduction). Trim them so the total stays under 3:00.
5. **Write the process paper** about what you actually did.
6. **Check the bibliography.** Keep only sources you really used, check each
   citation against the real source, and rewrite each annotation to say how
   you used it.
7. **Optional but strong:** interview a parent or grandparent about life
   before smartphones. An oral history counts as a primary source.

## How the word counter works

The counter runs when the page loads and updates the numbers on the Home page.
It counts every word inside the six content pages except quotes, source credits,
menus, page buttons, video slots, drawings, and builder notes. Turn on Builder
notes to see the count for each page.
